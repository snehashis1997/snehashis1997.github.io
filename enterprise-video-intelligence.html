Enterprise Video Intelligence for Workplace Safety & Security

Turning existing CCTV into a safety, security and operations system, using vision-language models instead of training a new detector for every use case.

Senior Research Engineer, Videonetics Technology Private Limited · Dec 2025 – Present

The problem

The client wanted dozens of new analytics across offices, kitchens, parking areas and secured rooms: slips and falls, blocked fire exits, open electrical panels, missing PPE, unattended items, wrong parking, meal-time crowding and more. Training a separate detector for each one would take months of labelling, and many of them (an "obstructed panel", a "wet polished floor") have no public dataset.

What I did

  • VLM analyzer engine. Each analytic is a carefully written question to Qwen3-VL-8B, served by vLLM with structured (yes/no) output. Video is cut into 20-second clips or 1-fps frames and cropped to user-drawn zones. An event needs a sustained run of "yes" answers in camera time, which keeps false alarms down. Prompts hot-reload, and every answer is logged with the prompt version that produced it.
  • Classic CV where it works better. LoFTR feature matching with RANSAC homography detects removed or displaced objects against a reference frame. SigLIP zero-shot attributes check PPE, hairnets, gloves and suspicious carry-outs on person tracks. Track-based rules handle the two-person rule, entry/exit counting, queue length and wrong-direction walking.
  • Natural-language video search. Operators type questions like "how many people in red shirts near gate 2 after 6 pm?". Four small models answer them: an intent router, an attribute tagger, a time-expression parser, and a narrative summarizer fine-tuned with LoRA from Qwen2.5-0.5B. There is no large-model call at query time, so it runs on CPU or about 3.4 GB of GPU. Held-out accuracy: 99.3% for intent and 0.998 F1 for 11-slot attribute extraction.
  • Predictive and multi-camera analytics. A Heat-Risk forecast of which cameras are likely to alert in the coming hours (backtested on a 7-day holdout), convoy detection from license-plate (ANPR) events across cameras, and VIP movement support.
  • Shipping. The work spans the analyzer microservices, Go API endpoints and web UI screens. Everything is packaged for offline, air-gapped installation on one 32 GB GPU, with model weights kept outside the images so they can be updated on their own.

Scope

  • Workplace safety: slip/fall, PPE, hygiene, blocked fire exits, electrical panel hazards.
  • Security: asset carry-out, object removal, two-person rule, weapons.
  • Operations: meal headcount, wrong parking, idle equipment.

Search-assistant figures are from held-out test data, not live site traffic. Heat-Risk, convoy and VIP analytics have been validated on synthetic data so far, with no field results yet.

What I learned

  • Designing prompts as specifications: precise, versioned and testable.
  • When a VLM beats a trained detector and when it doesn't. Geometry and matching problems went to LoFTR instead.
  • Making AI events trustworthy through temporal consistency rules rather than single-frame confidence.
